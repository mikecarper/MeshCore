# W12 crystal trim bench test — 3 November 2026

A W12 was tested against a T096 with a TCXO on an isolated 904.525 MHz,
62.5 kHz, SF7, CR 4/5 profile. Both used 96-symbol transmit preambles and
low transmit power. The antennas were moved over one metre apart. This is a
relative comparison, not a calibrated absolute frequency measurement.

With the W12 at its factory crystal trim and then at `3,3`, three T096
advertisements per setting were confirmed transmitted but the W12 did not
decode them. With `8,8`, the W12 decoded three advertisements matching the
T096's full public key. The fresh measured frequency errors were +1,426,
+1,422 and +1,419 Hz relative to the T096.

At `8,8`, three subsequent discovery exchanges passed with a matching W12
identity and fresh nonce. Each radio recorded exactly three receives and three
transmissions; neither recorded a new receive error during that trial.

Three additional peer comparisons at `8,8` each used three transmitted,
identity-matched and fresh advertisements. T096 readings were +1,770,
+1,735 and +1,712 Hz; T1000-E +915, +885 and +862 Hz; and MeshPocket
+1,850, +1,819 and +1,789 Hz. The mean of the three peer means was +1,482 Hz.
These are relative measurements from uncalibrated peers, and the receiving
harness matched the public key but did not independently reverify signatures.

Three more peers also showed positive offsets: RAK4631 +1,979 and +1,956 Hz
(a third observation was rejected as ambiguous), SolarXiao +1,911, +1,907
and +1,888 Hz, and XIAO nRF +1,857, +1,861 and +1,853 Hz. Their agreement
supports the direction of correction but does not establish an absolute
frequency reference.

In a separate automatic-calibration trial, the W12 received none of three
confirmed advertisements at factory trim or `3,3`. At `8,8`, three peers
were heard; neighboring settings produced larger errors. A second window
confirmed `8,8`, which was saved. Because the original setting received no
packets, improvement over its unknown error could not be measured.

A later three-peer run measured mean errors of +1,777, +1,795, and +1,611 Hz
with `8,8` and no digital adjustment. Automatic calibration saved a +1,379 ppb
correction after three independent verification observations measured +389,
+476 and +324 Hz. A subsequent capture measured +408/+392/+385 Hz,
+465/+454/+446 Hz, and +305/+293/+293 Hz, respectively. The adjustment is
relative to those transmitters and does not establish absolute transmit-frequency
accuracy. The trim and correction remained active after reflashing the final
test image and restarting. A fresh post-reboot capture measured +484/+461/+446,
+522/+507/+495, and +350/+343/+328 Hz from the same peers, respectively.

A separate receive-side check kept the T096 transmitter fixed and changed
only the W12's temporary tuning. Nominal W12 tuning measured +1,686, +1,651
and +1,636 Hz; tuning about 1 kHz higher measured +640, +621 and +617 Hz;
tuning about 1 kHz lower measured +2,582, +2,582 and +2,571 Hz. A final
nominal bracket measured +1,544, +1,541 and +1,537 Hz. All listed samples
were fresh and identity-matched. This verifies the receive-estimator direction
relative to that peer, not absolute transmit-frequency accuracy.

The W12 was left with `8,8` saved and its normal 910.525 MHz profile restored.
The former saved RXPS preference was switched off for the comparison; the
current LR2021 driver reports that RXPS is unsupported and rejected re-enabling
that preference. This trial does not measure current consumption or validate
temperature drift. One tested board and one reference cannot establish a
universal trim value.

The W12 ran a preview-based Repeater test image containing the detector-limit
fix. It connected to the local Wi-Fi network and to a temporary local MQTT
broker; MQTT reconnected after the broker was stopped and restarted. This does
not qualify the separately reported UniFi network. Temporary MQTT slot 3 was
disabled after the test; its inactive server and topic fields remain populated.
