    Five-board WiFi UDP bridge hardware qualification, 22 October 2026

    PASS for the tested Full Repeater configuration. Five physical ESP32 boards passed the directed UDP matrix, rejection checks, reconnects, software-reboot persistence and physical LoRa forwarding through eight configured UDP peers. All final images use source 36d9735e5. Independent raw-packet and cleanup audits passed before consolidation of the machine-readable results.

    Physical coverage combines two disjoint scopes: twelve accepted passing cases from failed run05 and eighteen from passing run07. Run05 remains failed; the first twelve cases were not repeated. Failed run06 sent no data and remains preserved separately.

    Coverage and results

    Check Completed result
    Canonical Full builds and installation Five boards; capability and memory gates passed; no feature reductions
    Directed UDP probes 300/300 across 60 directions, five probes each
    Security rejections 20/20: replay, bad authentication, unknown endpoint and malformed frame on each board
    Recovery WiFi reconnect and saved UDP-off choice passed on all five
    Qualified software reboots Five restarts, retained configuration and 20/20 fresh probes
    Physical LoRa delivery 25/25 exact application receipts, five per ingress board
    UDP-off negative controls 5/5; none crossed the frequency gap
    Independent copy audit 100 physical-peer copies and 100 Pi-endpoint copies; zero UDP transit
    Final cleanup Five boards quiet, original RXPS restored and owned fixture stopped

    The matrix covered 20 board-to-board, 20 board-to-Pi and 20 Pi-to-board directions. Initial station readiness was observed on the first read-only poll for every final image. Each board had 23 continuity samples spanning at least 1,043 seconds, with advancing uptime and no new latched error bits. The reboot qualification used deliberate software restarts, not cold power cycles. Identity, exact firmware/model, shared secret, UDP port and all eight peers persisted; eight authenticated sessions returned.

    The shared V4 target detects the V4.3 FEM at runtime. The Indicator tested here has 8 MiB flash and OPI PSRAM. Its separate 16 MiB/QSPI N16R2 recipe is not interchangeable and was not physically qualified. Existing display support was retained in the repeater recipes. Build receipts bind source, recipe and application/merged-image hashes, verify both OTA slot capacities, and report empty feature-reduction lists. Slot checks do not qualify an OTA installation.

    Physical method and operating conditions

    Each ESP32 had four other physical ESP32 peers plus four controlled Pi UDP endpoints: eight configured destinations, not eight physical peer radios. All joined an existing WiFi network as stations. MQTT, ESP-NOW and RS232 bridges were disabled. UDP used the existing bridge shared secret without another password; authentication does not encrypt the traffic.

    Physical trials used 917 MHz ingress and 918 MHz egress, SF7, 62.5 kHz bandwidth, CR5, preamble 32 and TX 2 dBm. Radio2 and LoRa RX powersaving were off during packet trials. Each ingress received one UDP-off control and five fresh positive frames. T096 and Tag served as the physical source/sink witnesses. Their different frequencies prevented a direct LoRa delivery from substituting for bridge forwarding. The Pi did not inject mesh data, and each accepted trial had exactly one physical send with no application retry.

    The independent audit checked source counters, exact application bytes and sequence, actual Pi event membership, four matching Pi copies per positive frame, all four physical-peer copy observations, and exactly one designated LoRa egress. Physical-peer observations include explicitly accounted duplicate receipts. No receiving UDP peer re-exported the frame to UDP. Before each role, the harness waited at least 65 seconds after radio activation and proved at least two seconds of stable RF/UDP counters.

    Historical watchdog flags and cumulative receive errors were retained in the raw baseline. V3 had four receive errors recorded during setup; no measured packet added one. A previously latched bit 8 was admitted only with explicit live RX history, matching diagnostic/physical error counters, soft-only recovery and an empty queue. This does not attribute every historical flag to idle maintenance. Every measured packet required unchanged latched flags and soft/hard recovery counters, zero new physical receive errors, and zero new witness receive errors. There was no blanket exemption for bit 8.

    All final core and memory queues were zero. V3 had no PSRAM; the other boards reported it. Cleanup restored all five original RXPS-on profiles, disabled UDP/repeat/logging, cleared all eight test peers, selected bridge source TX and left WebConfig inactive. Identity, saved radio, WiFi/secret and CPU power policy were preserved. No CPU-clock setter was issued; actual CPU-frequency readback is not claimed. Cleanup performed no flash, erase, reboot, stats clear or intentional data send. The final owned fixture stopped once, following the separately receipted stop of its predecessor.

    Fix and preserved failures

    The original V4.3 recovery failure exposed UDP/WebConfig station ownership: an unused automatic recovery AP prevented the saved station connection from returning. 36d9735e fixes that handoff while preserving explicit setup/OTA ownership and saved UDP-off intent. Twelve native ASan/UBSan tests and independent review preceded the five final builds; the final physical matrix then passed the formerly failing recovery sequence. The UDP protocol did not change.

    Earlier failures were retained rather than replaced by later passes. The history hash index records actual receipt outcomes and separate preserved archives.

    Preserved attempt Outcome and disposition
    Baseline matrix01 V4.3 recovery failed; XIAO recovery was not run. Final 36d9735e matrix02 passed all five.
    Initial Indicator join One WiFi off/on joined with the same saved credentials; separate root cause remains unproven.
    RF02 / RF03 Zero data trials: busy witness setter, then Pi USB/Ethernet host fault. Recovery03-01 readback adapter failed; corrected recovery03-02 passed all seven devices. This was not measured RF packet loss.
    RF04 One accepted negative request; automatic OTA bootstrap traffic changed the strict negative-control counters. A bounded bootstrap/quiet wait was added.
    RXPS restore01/03; setup04 V4.3 restoration rejected; setup04 separately failed on a startup queue. Restore02 and final cleanup restored all five original profiles. Ordinary V4.3 RXPS is supported.
    RF05 Twelve strict passes, then preflight stopped on a latched watchdog flag before V3 data. Overall failed status retained.
    RF06 Zero data; historical-baseline gate incorrectly required current last-RX age and absolute zero cumulative errors. Run07 retained baselines and enforced zero new errors per packet.
    Fixture and offline adapters Original fixture ended during the host fault; two explicit RF fixture replacements preserved identical configuration. Exact getter/model adapters were corrected without firmware changes or repeating accepted packets.

    Full CI is separate evidence. Test-only fixture repairs were pushed in 8da70b84d and 52a36c89d; focused tests passed. At report preparation, CI run 39869220511 had twelve successful non-main jobs while its main test was still running; full CI success is not asserted here.

    Evidence and limits

    The consolidated result binds all 32 opaque private-file hashes, both independent audits and the disjoint campaign receipts. Its SHA-256 is f6a384cc496f8c1a457ddd10df581925ca0a444ebad51fec65155fed3e68a4f3. The final private archive SHA-256 is 22adb7f49b6c1650248f63acf9f6f9a70971e5c7b3c3e17400c4b28803eb4206. That tar covers final evidence; earlier failures remain in separate preserved archives identified by the history index. Private contents are not published.

    The later noise-floor telemetry change in f4ade738d was not installed on these radios and is outside this hardware result.

    This qualifies Full Repeater UDP forwarding under the stated radio/power conditions. It does not qualify other firmware types, every power/radio profile, eight physical peers, AP-mode UDP, arbitrary router/VLAN policies or client-isolation bypass. Simultaneous bridge backends, BLE, physical firmware OTA application, cold starts, maximum load and long-duration soak remain outside this campaign.
