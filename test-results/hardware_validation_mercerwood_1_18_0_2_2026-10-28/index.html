    C9 is c9acec474. 70C7 is 70c7d5b47. 226 is 226ccc332. 70C7 changes the nRF52 emergency CPU idle backend; 226 changes the nRF52 Companion BLE lifecycle. V3 and V4.2 report the observer suffix in their runtime version. The RAK4631 hardware result is for its 70C7 Repeater image; the separate Full Companion capacity build and uninstalled S3 Observer build are described below.

    Measurements

    USB qualification requires 100 query cycles, ten fresh reopens and one verified warm reboot, with zero core flags. Companion checks cover owned contact creation/persistence/removal, natural expiry of a one-minute RAM-only radio2 lease, and restoration of the original secondary profile/automatic owner. Transmit-power and saved Bluetooth PIN setters require acknowledgement, readback, reopen and restoration. Actual RF power and an active PIN changing before restart were not measured.

    BLE requires three authenticated thirty-second sessions, the USB-bound public identity/device information and a clean HCI lifecycle. Payload delivery and strict HCI acceptance are separate results. T1000-E uses target-only USB host deauthorization; USB power remains uncommanded, so this is not a battery-only or physical cable-unplug test.

    On 226, Tag and MeshPocket pass both payload and strict HCI checks. T096 passes all three payload sessions and encryption succeeds three times, but its trace has an extra connection ending with 0x3e; strict acceptance therefore fails. No missing-key marker or host Bluetooth-service crash was observed in that trial, and its cause is unassigned.

    T1000-E's 226 trial actually released USB host ownership and waited ten seconds, leaving host WiFi unchanged. Its first phase completed no payload session: GATT reported Unlikely Error (0x0e), with HCI disconnect reasons 0x3e and 0x13. Encryption succeeded once; no missing-key marker or host daemon crash was observed. The second phase was not attempted after that failure. USB enumeration, identity, preferences, contacts, channels and uptime continuity were verified afterward, with zero core flags and queue length. No further BLE/RF retries were made.

    On C9, the T1000-E completed three payload sessions in its normal-host-WiFi trial, but the controller recorded an extra connection and disconnect reasons 0x3e / 0x22. A controlled WiFi-quiet trial completed no payload sessions, with 0x3e / 0x08; host WiFi and USB authorization were restored. Quieting WiFi did not establish a remedy. A separate radio2-off diagnostic found no unique matching advertisement within its bounded scan, so it attempted no connection and captured no authentication failure under that condition.

    The V4.3 initially had a host/device bond mismatch. Target-only host bond repair later yielded three clean thirty-second sessions with the original saved PIN restored. A temporary-PIN phase failed while BlueZ 5.66 crashed during notification setup; that failure remains recorded. Identity, settings, contacts, channels and unrelated host bonds were checked afterward. This is a conditional C9 pass; no firmware remedy for the host crash is claimed.

    RF trials use shipping firmware, zero-hop controls, fresh nonces, full peer identity and physical TX/RX counters. Live control pushes leave the offline message queue untouched. Bench RF/TX settings and owned awake holds are restored. Repeater discovery checks the radio2 extension within the existing four-per-two-minute limiter.

    The fresh 226 Tag star completed all 18 legs: three exchanges in each direction with T1000-E, T096 and MeshPocket. Every leg matched the identity, nonce and physical packet counters, with zero receive errors; the slowest took 0.524 seconds. Original radio/TX settings, binary preferences, contacts and channels were restored, with no offline-queue reads or RF retries. Earlier C9 exchanges remain separate history. V3, T-Deck, Indicator and RAK3401 had discovery hidden by their saved advert settings. Their conditional trials temporarily enabled discovery on the isolated bench profile and restored the original values. A missing response while discovery was disabled is not a failed response from an eligible node.

    The Tower returned two of three baseline requests, one of two quiet, paced requests, and one of two requests with RX power saving disabled. Missing responses remain unresolved; disabling RXPS did not establish a remedy. Original settings and its automatic emergency mode with no awake hold were restored after each measured trial.

    Separate reviews accepted the original V4.3 six exchanges and XIAO nRF52840 three responses: their harness failures compared changing timing/countdown or live battery fields as saved settings. Read-only checks confirmed restoration; no RF was repeated. Original failures remain recorded. Preflight formatter/type errors sent no requests. A manual V3 diagnostic exposed an unsafe host transport fallback; recovery completed and the harness now requires an explicit reviewed transport before opening a port.

    Production fixes

    The Tower reported 0% battery and entered automatic emergency radio sleep. The first candidate's ten-second CPU idle slices starved USB cleanup/reply draining: queries and three reopens passed, but the fourth received no reply. Its original application and queried configuration were restored before the fix.

    70C7 caps CPU idle slices at 100 ms while a USB host is enumerated, retaining the ten-second detached limit. It does not wake LoRa or change the battery policy. On the Tower, automatic mode, reported 0%, sleeping radio and no awake override were verified before and after USB stress, and again after warm reboot. All 213 completed commands responded, with a maximum measured latency of 1.0952 seconds. The backend's five tests and runtime's thirteen tests pass with address and undefined-behavior sanitizers. Actual battery state of charge and current draw were not measured.

    Earlier emergency-power checks retain their original source; this campaign adds the automatic-entry USB regression/retest.

    A host regression reproduced a BLE connection stuck after USB disabled/re-enabled Bluetooth while a callback was delayed. 226 rejects stale physical sessions, uses bounded disconnect recovery and preserves a replacement connection when an old disconnect arrives. 25 focused native tests passed, covering delayed callbacks, normal sessions, retry timing and advertising recovery. Four Full nRF52 Companion packages pass capability checks with unchanged linked RAM admission. All four pass their new 100-query, ten-reopen and warm-reboot checks; all 18 new RF legs also pass. Tag/Pocket BLE passes and T096's strict lifecycle exception are reported separately above. The earlier T1000-E failures remain causally unassigned.

    Emergency scheduling and collector

    On 70C7, SolarXiao MPPT 33S and RAK3401 completed a real one-minute awake lease, two accelerated past-RTC hourly wake boundaries and manual-mode persistence through a verified warm reboot. Independent advertisements proved timer wake after approximately 22 seconds without a target USB write at each boundary. The CPU remained connected to an enumerated USB host; these are RF scheduling measurements, not detached-host or current-consumption measurements.

    Solar's first manual-reboot check closed CDC before the command was consumed. Its successful lease/timer-wake observations remain separate from that failed axis. The corrected single-write driver holds CDC until reset or 1.5 seconds; a focused follow-up proved reduced uptime and manual persistence without repeating the wake trials. RAK3401 completed the corrected full trial.

    Present UTC was restored before original RF settings, and owned awake holds were revoked. A separate full-body read-only comparison verified two Solar ACL entries and one RAK3401 ACL entry, available saved settings and trusted OTA signer lists. Earlier first-line-only ACL counts are superseded by that complete comparison. Solar's observed CAD flag and the quiet watchdog flags on some boards remain in the preparation history; warm preparation is not counted as a clean long-duration run.

    The collector's five configuration/unit digests, original active state and serial ownership were restored. One owned RAW packet was observed in its journal with receive metadata and a successful local MQTT publish call. Receipt at an external broker was not measured.

    Builds and limits

    The Indicator's first candidate readiness check received no reply within six seconds. Its original application and data were restored and verified before one controlled trial of the same candidate. That trial received the exact version and original identity after 29.3055 seconds, then passed the unchanged 100-query / ten-reopen / warm-reboot qualification with zero core flags. The startup wait is preparation, not extra accepted query cycles; the original failure and each unsuccessful readiness probe remain recorded.

    The initial 15 board plans / 20 packaged profiles pass RAM/capability checks, including Full/Reduced images for five nRF52 Repeaters. Four replacement Full Companion packages were checked for 226. The provisional XIAO ESP32-S3 Companion target is superseded by an uninstalled Observer Repeater build. PlatformIO ran serially. Full application installs omit bootloader, SoftDevice, filesystem and partition-table images. ESP32 recovery restored original applications and NVS/SPIFFS snapshots with readback verification.

    An additional RAK4631 Full Companion build at f6034e1f, using the CI library versions and maximum development-version length, passes capacity checks with a 712,652-byte packaged application in 712,704 bytes: 52 bytes free. Its final change shortens Bluetooth startup diagnostics. This is a build-only result; that Companion image was not installed on the tested Repeater. The S3 Observer Repeater package at 5a72a991 also passes build checks and remains uninstalled.

    The user explicitly made original-image backups optional for these test radios. RAK4631 therefore received its verified 70C7 application through working CDC DFU without an original rollback image. It passed 100 queries, ten reopens, warm reboot, exact candidate image/identity checks and current-RTC readiness; its final core flags and queue were zero. Seven available original settings and the OTA signer list matched. Its older firmware returned ??: for password, ACL and several newer getters, so their preservation cannot be established from those queries. The original comparison failure remains recorded; a narrow legacy-error classification review and fresh readiness check accepted the completed measurements without reflashing or repeating them. Bootloader version text changed from its numeric fallback to OTAFIX2.4.12; no bootloader image was written, and a before/after CRC-equality claim is not made.

    RAK4631's later RF preflight refused a latched idle watchdog flag 8 before sending any request. One documented warm reboot reduced uptime from 2,545 to 4 seconds; the unchanged 70C7 image then returned all three exact discovery responses to the 226 Tag observer, including the matching radio2 extension. Fifteen available current settings, one current ACL entry and OTA signers matched across that warm preparation. This does not establish the unavailable pre-upgrade password/ACL state or long-duration idle reliability.

    XIAO ESP32-S3 did not answer application probes or the programmer after two physical ROM-entry attempts. No firmware was written. A MAC-bound DHCP/neighbour lookup found no address, so no HTTP identification probe was made. It remains unqualified; an enumerated USB device alone does not prove a working application.

    The remaining release gates are the Tower's missing discovery responses, T096's extra BLE connection, T1000-E's failed BLE phase and the unqualified S3. Successful checks on other interfaces do not clear those results.

    Read-only OTA readiness is distinct from an actual LoRa firmware transfer and installation. These checks do not establish range, calibrated transmit power, display pixel quality, cold-power recovery, phone interoperability, current savings, long-duration reliability or every firmware feature.
