    RAK3401 radio2 fast switching, 23 October 2026

    Status: PARTIAL. Switching improved; the 500 us maximum goal is not met. The production candidate retains buffered 8 MHz SPI on SPI1/SPIM2. Private 16 MHz experiments failed radio initialization or returned incorrect radio readback. They are not production defaults. Fresh witnessed RF delivery qualification failed: the scanner received 195 of 200 test packets.

    Timing checkpoints

    These are hardware measurements of the RX-to-RX profile-switch path, including admission, standby, changed radio parameters and restarting receive. They are not bare PLL-lock times. The production budget uses the sampled maximum plus a rounded-up 10% margin; it is not a guarantee against a larger future sample.

    Source Change Sampled maximum, us Guarded budget, us
    adb11e7b Original 2 MHz, byte-at-a-time SPI 1069 1176
    99979655 Buffered 2 MHz SPI 855 941
    258b37df Buffered 2 MHz plus modem/IQ caches 702 773
    b8b7880a Buffered 8 MHz plus caches 550 605
    1b97c089a Normal 8 MHz production image, longer RF campaign 611 673

    The ordinary nRF52 switch budget uses the 32,768 Hz RTC2 clock, approximately 30.52 us per tick. A separate 64 MHz DWT instrumentation build from 2629c158 captured 586 successful switches in each direction:

    Direction Minimum, us Mean, us Maximum, us
    Primary to secondary 410 415.643 555
    Secondary to primary 409 414.614 550

    Both directions recorded zero busy admissions and zero failed switches in that capture. DWT stage counters, later interrupt observers and experimental guards add work. Times from differently instrumented builds are not directly comparable with each other or with production timing. A mean below 500 us does not satisfy the requested maximum.

    Why 16 MHz is not enabled

    The pure Repeater failed startup at 16 MHz on SPIM3 with RXDELAY settings 0, 1 and 2. Read-only SWD captures showed completed SPI frames, SoftDevice disabled, and no observed fault-register evidence in those snapshots. This does not support blaming those particular failures on Bluetooth or USB enumeration. It also does not establish a hard clock limit for the pins.

    A narrower diagnostic initialized the radio successfully at 8 MHz, then issued 16 read-only GetPacketType commands at each of 8, 16 and 8 MHz. No radio writes or resets occurred between phases. The controller, pins and explicit anomaly-198 workaround remained the same; the per-frame SoftDevice safety check remained active.

    RXDELAY Initial 8 MHz correct / 16 16 MHz correct / 16 Restored 8 MHz correct / 16
    2 16 4 16
    1 16 3 16
    0 16 2 16

    The first two raw response bytes, A2,A2, are normal for these reads. The failure is the final packet-type byte differing from the expected LoRa value 01. Restoring 8 MHz restored all 16 correct reads in every trial. This isolates a reproducible fast-read problem after successful initialization; it does not identify whether its cause is signal timing, the controller or another part of the SPI path. No 16 MHz shipping claim is made.

    Experimental scheduling and interrupt controls

    Task-priority elevation, bounded USB interrupt deferral and their explicitly enabled combination were slower in the measured builds. All remain private, default-off HIL options. The combined experiment did not achieve the 500 us maximum goal.

    One combined worst-hop capture reported zero activity for all seven observed interrupt/scheduler sources. Shared TWI0/SPIM0 coverage was then added in aa480ae79; the exact linked vectors and scheduler call were verified before the physical test. Both directions completed 591 successful switches with no failed switches; the reverse direction also recorded 12 busy admissions. The slowest forward switch took 659.109 us and again recorded zero activity, now across all eight observed sources. The reverse maximum was 575.234 us. SysTick ENABLE and TICKINT were both off when read after stopping the test. These observations rule out attribution to the recorded sources for that forward hop, without proving a particular memory, peripheral or CPU-stall cause. Observer overhead and inclusive handler timing also prevent treating these counters as a complete or additive explanation of elapsed time. The observer excludes architectural exception paths and the experiment's RTC2 deadline handler; the USB guard recorded no deadline expiry. The retained worst sample is selected by integer microseconds, so equal-microsecond ties can differ in raw cycles.

    Production scope and validation

    The RAK3401 production path uses buffered SPI1/SPIM2 at 8 MHz. It does not enable the global nrfx SPIM3 anomaly-198 workaround, which is unsafe with an enabled SoftDevice. The newly buffered, unqualified nRF default-SPI/SPIM3 constructor rollout was restored to its prior defaults in 4e513b19d. Previously buffered LR1110 targets retain their prior constructors. Shared cache improvements and the ESP32/RP2040 improvements remain. SenseCAP Indicator is excluded, and MeshPocket has not been physically tested in this campaign.

    Focused native checks passed for buffered transfers and constructor/bus selection, cache invalidation, CPU-clock policy transitions, timing captures, SoftDevice refusal, read-probe timeout/restoration/recovery decisions, and default-off experiment gates. Relevant native fixtures execute the actual helpers; sanitizer checks cover the diagnostic paths. These checks do not replace hardware qualification.

    Cross-architecture build receipts include RAK3401, RAK4631, ThinkNode M4, Heltec V2/V3, ESP32-C6 and Pico W targets. They cover campaign checkpoints, not every board, firmware role or final-source image. The raw Station_G2_repeater PlatformIO target exceeded its default 1,310,720-byte application limit both at the baseline and after the changes; that pre-existing overflow remains a separate build limitation. No all-builds or all-boards release qualification is claimed here.

    Most held nRF52 radio buses use SPIM3. Its anomaly-198 register workaround conflicts with an enabled SoftDevice. Moving to SPIM2 also needs an ownership audit: RAK4631 SD recipes reserve it for the RAK15002, while several display boards already use SPI1/SPIM2. Single-interface counts alone do not prove that the controller is free. Indicator remains excluded, and MeshPocket remains outside physical testing.

    The inventory exposed one stale LR1110 test that still required the restored ThinkNode M4 buffered constructor. Its expectation now preserves both the shared fast RX state and the intentionally conservative M4 transport. The LR1110 and actual nRF52/RP2040 constructor suites passed all 11 focused tests; this correction changes no firmware behavior.

    Fresh witnessed RF test

    The normal production image from 1b97c089a was installed and its running body hash matched the retained Full ELF/package. Two fixed receivers witnessed the same 200 fresh, one-shot test packets, with no test-packet retransmission. The settled recommendation was 32/192 symbols; the test selected 32/200.

    Receiver Primary received / sent Secondary received / sent
    RAK3401 scanning both profiles 96 / 100 99 / 100
    Fixed primary witness 100 / 100 -
    Fixed secondary witness - 100 / 100

    There were no duplicate test packets, wrong-profile receipts, collector drops, new core error flags, watchdog recoveries or switch-command errors. The RAK recorded one additional receive error. The five-minute nonhalting CPU-status capture passed its integrity gates: 27,134 samples, maximum gap 17 ms, no CPU halt/debug enable, reset or lockup in the observation window. USB was active, so this does not qualify CPU sleep or electrical power consumption.

    The gateway and RAK settings were restored. The sender's original transmit power restoration initially returned a radio-busy error; a separate settings- only cleanup continuation was required. The original failed receipt was preserved. Packet delivery qualification and the 500 us goal both remain failed; an offline audit independently reproduced the same five missing packets.

    Remaining work

    The earlier witnessed RF run lost three primary packets while its fixed witness received all 100. A recommended-preamble run also had scanner losses. The new test confirms that scanner packet loss remains after these speed improvements. Faster switching alone has not fixed delivery.

    The additional interrupt observer has completed its physical test. The normal production image was built separately from 1b97c089a, with all HIL helpers absent and SPI1/SPIM2 selected; the later observer change affects HIL only. Fresh witnessed RF testing of that production image is complete and failed. The remaining work is to fix packet loss, explain and reduce the remaining long switching intervals, and requalify the changed implementation. The 500 us maximum remains unmet.

    Machine-readable sanitized results record the same scope and failures.
